All numbers below are **measured** and copied from files generated by `python scripts/analyze.py`
from [`results/raw/`](../results/raw/) only. Unlinked repository paths in this document are
relative to the project root:

- Tables: [`results/processed/analysis__20261018T222449Z/`](../results/processed/analysis__20261018T222449Z/) (`main_table`, `reference_accuracy`,
  `two_vs_four`, `similarity`, `similarity_vs_retention`, `runtime_memory`, `hypotheses.md`,
  `summary.json`, and `sources.json` with the SHA-256 of every raw file used).
- Figures: [`figures/analysis__20261018T222449Z/`](../figures/analysis__20261018T222449Z/) (`per_task_accuracy.png`,
  `two_vs_four_retention.png`, `similarity_matrix.png`, `similarity_vs_retention.png`).

Setting for every run: CLIP ViT-B/32, `canonical-v1` (TA λ 0.3; TIES λ 0.3, top 20%, sum;
DOGE TA η 0.07, subspace 6, K 30, Adam 1e-4 × 400), seed 42, test splits, one RTX 4090.
Accuracy and retention are in %. Retention = 100 × merged accuracy / individual-expert accuracy.

## 1. Findings by research question

**RQ1: DOGE versus Task Arithmetic and TIES.** DOGE TA has the highest mean accuracy and the
highest worst-task accuracy in all seven merges. Gain in mean accuracy over Task Arithmetic:
+3.60 (P01) to +9.47 points (F01); over TIES: +12.76 (P01) to +22.77 points (P04). Mean
normalized retention of DOGE is 96.74-99.23 on the six pairs and 94.66 on the four-task merge.
TIES is the weakest method in every merge (see section 3, D-TIES).

**RQ2: two versus four models** (`two_vs_four`, [protocol §5.3](EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md#53-analysis-definitions)). Change in mean
retention from the pair average to F01: Task Arithmetic −6.05 points (91.13 → 85.08),
DOGE TA −3.24 (97.89 → 94.66), TIES +0.73 (79.35 → 80.08). DOGE loses retention on all four
tasks but at most 4.96 points (EuroSAT); Task Arithmetic loses most on EuroSAT (−20.22) and
GTSRB (−6.07) and gains slightly on MNIST and SVHN. In F01 the weakest task is EuroSAT for Task
Arithmetic (72.67) and GTSRB for TIES (70.58) and DOGE (91.35).

**RQ3: similarity versus mergeability** (n = 6 pairs, descriptive only). Spearman's ρ between
cosine similarity and mean retention is +0.03 (Task Arithmetic), −0.20 (TIES) and −0.03
(DOGE TA): no consistent rank relationship. The most similar pair (MNIST-SVHN, 0.154) ranks 2nd,
2nd and 3rd in retention; the least similar (MNIST-EuroSAT, 0.013) ranks 1st for every method.
What the ranks do show: the three pairs containing MNIST are the top three in retention for
every method, so in this task set **which tasks are merged** (MNIST is retained almost
perfectly) matters more than how similar their task vectors are. With six pairs and cosines
all between 0.013 and 0.154 this is an observation, not a tested effect.

## 3. Discrepancy analysis

- **References match the paper.** Every individual expert is within 0.63 points and the
  pretrained model within 0.50 points of the paper-reported Table 1
  ([`configs/paper_reference.yaml`](../configs/paper_reference.yaml)).
- **Similarity matches the paper.** Our six cosines (0.154, 0.065, 0.051, 0.026, 0.025, 0.013)
  agree with the paper-reported Fig. 2(a) values (0.15, 0.07, 0.05, 0.03, 0.02, 0.01) in size and
  order, apart from the near tie GTSRB-EuroSAT / SVHN-EuroSAT.
- **D-TIES: TIES below Task Arithmetic.** On the paper's 8-task benchmark TIES beats Task
  Arithmetic (paper-reported 72.4 vs 69.1). Here TIES is below in every merge: 9.1-15.0 points on
  the six pairs and 4.9 points on F01. Likely
  cause, not tested: λ = 0.3 was taken from the 8-task benchmark and fixed for all task sets; TIES
  keeps only 20% of each task vector, so with 2 or 4 tasks its merged vector is much smaller than
  Task Arithmetic's at the same λ. TIES's slight *gain* from two to four tasks fits that
  explanation (more tasks → larger summed vector). The official `merge_func: sum` (D4) and the
  absence of per-task-set λ tuning are part of this. λ was not tuned on test data to "fix" it.
- **DOGE implementation differences** ([PAPER_TO_CODE_MAP.md](PAPER_TO_CODE_MAP.md)): shared subspace of rank/6 independent of n
  (D1), row-wise objective (D2), only the 72 linear weight matrices change while TA/TIES change all
  parameters (D5), and Adam letting 25-44% of Δ's norm into the shared subspace although every
  gradient is projected out of it (D7). Results are for the official code as written.

## 4. Limitations

1. **Fixed λ for the baselines.** Task Arithmetic and TIES normally tune λ on validation data per
   task set; `canonical-v1` fixes λ = 0.3 for every merge. This likely understates TIES (D-TIES)
   and possibly Task Arithmetic on two-task merges. DOGE's η is also fixed, so the protocol is
   consistent, but the baseline gap would probably shrink with tuning.
2. **Small task set.** Four tasks, six pairs, one four-task merge. The similarity analysis rests
   on six points with a narrow cosine range (0.013-0.154) and is confounded by task identity
   (MNIST pairs retain most). No statistical claim is made.
3. **One seed.** All methods are deterministic given the checkpoints (BASE-03 confirmed identical
   weights on repeat); no variance across seeds or fine-tuning runs is captured.
4. **Checkpoints.** The experts are FusionBench's Hugging Face ports (`tanganke/*`), not the
   original Task Arithmetic checkpoints the paper used; references match the paper within 0.63
   points. The model cards state no licence; GTSRB and EuroSAT dataset cards state none either.
5. **Cost measurements.** DOGE merges take 45-72 s versus 4-12 s for the baselines and use
   1.75-1.82 GB of GPU memory; Task Arithmetic and TIES run on CPU, so their GPU peak is 0 and only
   process RSS is reported (TIES F01 peaks at 12.4 GB RAM). One RTX 4090, single runs; timings
   include model loading inside `method.run` and the probes' small overhead.
6. **Implementation-level deviations from the paper's description** (D1, D2, D5, D7) apply to
   every DOGE number. DOGE-03 passes with a float32 tolerance of 1e-3 rather than 1e-5.
7. **Infrastructure.** Runs were made on a Runpod pod without persistent storage; every raw result
   was copied to the Mac immediately and the pod was then stopped. Reproducing needs a fresh
   environment ([`scripts/setup_env.sh`](../scripts/setup_env.sh)) and the pinned revisions in
   [`configs/pins.yaml`](../configs/pins.yaml); see [INSTRUCTION.md §6](../INSTRUCTION.md#6-re-run-the-experiments-gpu).
